ABOUT THE PROJECT

Diabetes Risk Assessment

An applied data analytics project using 2025 CDC BRFSS data to explore diabetes risk factors and predict reported diabetes status.

About the Project

This project was developed as part of the CSIS 4495 Applied Research course. The goal is to combine data analytics, machine learning, data visualization, and web development into a practical diabetes risk assessment application.

The application allows users to enter selected demographic, health, and lifestyle information and receive a model-generated classification of No Diabetes, Prediabetes, or Diabetes.

Why Study Diabetes Risk?

Diabetes is a major chronic health condition that can affect many parts of the body. Over time, poorly controlled diabetes can contribute to complications involving the cardiovascular system, kidneys, nerves, and eyes.

Prediabetes is also important because it may indicate increased risk of developing type 2 diabetes. Identifying patterns associated with diabetes and prediabetes can help support earlier awareness and preventive health discussions.

This project therefore focuses not only on predicting reported diabetes status, but also on exploring how demographic, socioeconomic, health, and lifestyle factors are associated with diabetes patterns in a large population dataset.

Data Source

The analysis uses the 2025 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) public-use dataset from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

BRFSS is a large-scale health survey containing information about health conditions, health-related behaviors, demographic characteristics, and access to healthcare.

After data preparation and selection, the analytical dataset used in this project contains approximately 352,705 records.

Prediction Model

A weighted logistic regression model was selected as the final prediction model. Survey weights from BRFSS were incorporated during model development to better reflect the survey design.

The model uses 13 predictors:

Age Sex BMI High Blood Pressure Smoking Status High Cholesterol Physical Activity Education Income Group Race / Ethnicity Fruit Intake Vegetable Intake General Health

The model classifies each assessment into one of three categories: No Diabetes, Prediabetes, or Diabetes.

Data Analysis & Visualization

Microsoft Power BI was used to explore patterns in the BRFSS data and create visualizations covering overall diabetes prevalence, demographic patterns, health risk factors, socioeconomic and lifestyle characteristics, model performance, and key predictors.

The dashboard includes analysis of factors such as age, BMI, sex, income, education, smoking, physical activity, fruit and vegetable intake, and reported general health.

Limitations

BRFSS is primarily based on self-reported survey information. Self-reported health conditions and behaviors may be affected by recall, awareness, reporting differences, and other sources of bias.

In particular, the diabetes outcome used in this project reflects reported diabetes or prediabetes status rather than laboratory measurements such as blood glucose or A1C results.

The model therefore identifies statistical patterns within the BRFSS dataset and should not be interpreted as a clinical diagnostic tool.
